Of 81 Florida banks and credit unions, 54 already protect their customers' connections against
future quantum decryption. 27 do not yet.

Nothing is broken today. Every connection we tested is encrypted with algorithms no existing
computer can break.

The risk is time. Traffic recorded now can be decrypted once a large enough quantum computer
exists, and in 2025 the estimated machine needed shrank from about twenty million qubits to under
one million. NIST has set 2030 to begin retiring today's key exchange and 2035 to end it. Your
browser already protects traffic the new way.

The fix is small. For 10 institutions the next step is to enable TLS 1.3; for the other 17 it is
one setting, the post-quantum key exchange their servers and CDNs already support.

We published the full note, every institution named with its result and a way to re-test it at
once, and the scanner behind it is free and open source. Type any host name and see where it
stands in a second.

Covered by Quantum Zeitgeist; carried by Yahoo Finance and Morningstar.
